Bolsonaro’s AI Avatar: Political Surrogate or Legal Loophole in Brazil’s Election

Overview

Former Brazilian President Jair Bolsonaro, barred from running in this year’s election due to legal convictions, has turned to artificial intelligence to maintain political visibility. Through a lifelike AI avatar, Bolsonaro has appeared at campaign events, delivered speeches, and sustained his movement’s presence despite being under house arrest. This development raises urgent questions about the boundaries of electoral law, the role of synthetic media in democracy, and whether digital representations can serve as legal substitutes for disqualified candidates.

Key Insights & Concepts

AI as a Political Surrogate: A Legal Gray Zone

Bolsonaro’s use of an AI avatar is not merely a publicity stunt — it represents a deliberate attempt to bypass electoral disqualification. Courts and election authorities now face the unprecedented task of determining whether a digital entity can fulfill the role of a human candidate under existing laws, which were written long before generative AI existed.

Transparency vs. Deception: Intent Matters

Unlike malicious deepfakes designed to mislead voters, Bolsonaro’s avatar appears to be used openly and declaratively — stating its artificial nature while still conveying political messages. This distinction complicates regulation: should the law focus on the technology’s potential to deceive, or its function as a tool for political persistence?

State-Level Action Outpaces Federal Response

While 26 U.S. states have enacted laws targeting deepfakes in political ads, Brazil and many other democracies lack specific rules governing AI avatars of barred politicians. The Bolsonaro case may become a global precedent, prompting nations to reconsider what constitutes “campaigning” when the candidate is not physically present.

The Movement, Not the Man: AI as Symbolic Continuity

The avatar’s repeated message — that prison cannot silence the movement — shifts the focus from individual candidacy to ideological persistence. This suggests that AI may not be used to replace a person, but to perpetuate a political brand or movement, raising questions about whether electoral bans can ever truly suppress dissent when technology enables symbolic continuity.

Takeaways & Next Steps

Urgent Need for AI-Specific Electoral Regulations

Existing election laws are inadequate to address AI avatars, synthetic candidates, or digital surrogates. Legislators must draft clear rules defining what constitutes political campaigning when mediated by AI — including disclosure requirements, limitations on use for disqualified individuals, and penalties for evasion.

Platforms Must Implement Detection and Labeling Systems

Social media and broadcasting platforms should deploy AI detection tools and mandate clear labeling of synthetic political content. Just as sponsored content requires disclosure, AI-generated political appearances should be visibly marked to preserve voter autonomy and informed consent.

Civil Society Must Monitor and Document AI Use in Politics

Watchdog organizations, journalists, and academics should track instances of AI avatars, deepfakes, and synthetic media in electoral contexts globally. A public registry of such uses would help identify patterns, assess risks, and inform future policy responses before misuse becomes widespread.

Prepare for a New Era of Digital Political Presence

The Bolsonaro case is likely just the beginning. As AI avatars become more realistic and accessible, we may see more barred leaders, exiled figures, or even deceased politicians “returning” via synthetic media. Societies must decide now whether to embrace this as a form of free expression or treat it as a threat to democratic legitimacy — and act accordingly.
