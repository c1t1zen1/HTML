Funding, basis, and liquidity are the stress variables

Mudrex explicitly lists leverage-amplified losses, funding-rate manipulation, liquidation cascades during volatility, and regulatory uncertainty around derivative classification as risks. These risks make funding conditions and forced deleveraging central to any evaluation of a perpetual-futures bot.

The AEA program snippet adds a basis-risk perspective: increasing liquidity-trader risk bearing can also raise volatility. The supplied evidence therefore links the evaluation to more than an isolated funding-rate number; it also points toward the interaction among basis conditions, liquidity, and volatility. The packet does not provide enough detail to quantify that interaction.

  • Funding-rate manipulation is identified as a specific risk, not merely a theoretical performance variable.
  • Leverage can amplify losses when volatility increases.
  • Liquidation cascades are an explicit failure mode to test.
  • Basis and liquidity conditions may coincide with higher volatility.

FIELD NOTE

Controls matter more than headline performance

The practical guidance in the packet emphasizes controllability: auditability, risk limits, paper trading, and small-size live testing are presented as preferable to flashy AI claims. It also recommends separating agentic research and alerts from tightly constrained execution, using trading-only API permissions, strict whitelists, and no withdrawal access.

Those controls do not demonstrate profitability, but they define a safer way to test the unanswered question. A credible evaluation would need to observe the bot through changing funding and basis conditions while preserving an auditable record of decisions, limits, execution, and losses. The supplied sources support that discipline but do not report its results for any named bot.

  • Require auditability and explicit risk limits before treating performance claims as credible.
  • Progress from backtesting to paper trading and then tiny-size live testing.
  • Keep execution tightly constrained and use trading-only permissions.
  • Evaluate performance specifically across funding, basis, volatility, and liquidation conditions.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence supports a cautious conclusion: AI bots may automate execution, risk management, and rapid responses, but the supplied sources do not establish that they maintain returns during funding-rate or futures-basis shocks. The most relevant risks—funding-rate manipulation, leverage-amplified losses, liquidation cascades, basis risk, and volatility—are identified, while bot-specific stress-test results are missing.

Positive performance claims should therefore be treated as hypotheses or forecasts until supported by transparent, out-of-sample testing across stressed perpetual-market regimes. The strongest practical path in the supplied material is controlled deployment: auditability, explicit limits, paper trading, tiny-size live tests, and restricted execution permissions.
