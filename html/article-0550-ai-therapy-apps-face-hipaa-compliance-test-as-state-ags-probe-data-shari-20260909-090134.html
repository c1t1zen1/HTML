AI Therapy Apps Face HIPAA-Compliance Test as State AGs Probe Data-Sharing Practices

State attorneys general are investigating AI therapy apps for deceptive marketing and data sharing, signaling a shift from federal clinical validation to aggressive consumer protection enforcement.

September 9, 2026 · 9:01 AM PDT · 4 min read · 12 sources · Original family: AI in Mental Health & Therapy
AI TherapyData PrivacyState Enforcement
Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

Enforcement against AI mental health tools is rapidly shifting from federal clinical oversight to state-level consumer protection actions. Recent investigations by Texas and multi-state coalitions target companies like Meta and Character.AI for allegedly misleading users about their therapeutic capabilities and mishandling sensitive health data. This trend highlights a growing regulatory gap where existing privacy and fraud laws are being weaponized against AI chatbots that operate in clinical-adjacent spaces without proper licensure or transparency. The evidence suggests that state attorneys general are becoming the primary enforcers in this sector, leveraging civil investigative demands to scrutinize data practices and marketing claims.

Assignment angle: Focuses on the emerging enforcement trend of state attorneys general investigating mental health AI apps for sharing user data with third-party advertisers, distinct from federal FDA clinical validation or California-specific companion rules.

FIELD NOTE

Texas Leads the Charge Against Deceptive AI Mental Health Claims

Texas Attorney General Ken Paxton has initiated civil investigative demands against Meta AI Studio and Character.AI, alleging that these platforms deceive consumers by marketing themselves as mental health tools. The investigation focuses on whether these companies violate Texas consumer protection laws by making fraudulent claims and misrepresenting their privacy practices. Specifically, the AG alleges that these platforms impersonate licensed mental health professionals while potentially harvesting user data without adequate disclosure. This action sets a precedent for treating AI chatbots as regulated health services under consumer fraud statutes rather than just general software products.

  • Civil Investigative Demands issued to Meta AI Studio and Character.AI in August 2025.
  • Allegations include impersonating licensed mental health professionals and deceptive marketing.
  • Focus on violations of Texas laws prohibiting fraudulent claims and privacy misrepresentations.

Multi-State Coalitions Expand Scrutiny to Health Data and Minors

Beyond Texas, a broader coalition of state attorneys general is coordinating efforts to address the risks posed by AI chatbots, particularly regarding minors and vulnerable populations. A bipartisan group led by Pennsylvania, New Jersey, West Virginia, and Massachusetts AGs has demanded safeguards from major AI software companies, including robust safety testing and clear consumer warnings. Additionally, a multi-state probe into OpenAI has emerged, with subpoenas targeting the company’s handling of health data and advertising policies. These coordinated actions indicate a national strategy to use existing privacy and consumer protection laws to regulate AI interactions that mimic therapeutic relationships.

  • Coalition of 42 AGs led by PA, NJ, WV, and MA demanding AI safeguards.
  • OpenAI facing multi-state subpoenas regarding health data and ad policies.
  • Focus on protecting minors and vulnerable users from harmful AI interactions.

State AGs Emerge as Primary AI Enforcers Amid Federal Gridlock

With federal regulatory frameworks for AI still developing, state attorneys general are filling the enforcement vacuum by applying traditional consumer protection, privacy, and civil rights statutes to AI technologies. Legal analysts note that this approach allows states to act swiftly against specific harms, such as data sharing with third-party advertisers or deceptive marketing, without waiting for comprehensive federal legislation. The enforcement trend is not limited to California-specific rules but represents a nationwide movement where state AGs are actively investigating how AI companies handle sensitive user information and whether they mislead consumers about the nature of their services.

  • State AGs using consumer protection and privacy laws as primary AI regulatory tools.
  • Enforcement actions targeting data sharing practices and deceptive marketing.
  • Shift away from reliance on federal FDA clinical validation for chatbot apps.

Closing read

Conclusion

The regulatory landscape for AI mental health applications is being reshaped by aggressive state-level enforcement rather than federal clinical approval processes. By leveraging civil investigative demands and consumer protection laws, state attorneys general are directly challenging the business models of AI companies that offer therapeutic-like services. This shift places significant legal risk on platforms that fail to clearly disclose the non-clinical nature of their tools or that share sensitive user data with third parties. As these investigations progress, they establish a de facto compliance standard for AI therapy apps, emphasizing transparency and data privacy over clinical validation.

Five takeaways

  • Texas AGs are actively investigating AI chatbots for deceptive mental health marketing.
  • Multi-state coalitions are targeting health data handling and minor safety.
  • State AGs are currently the primary enforcers of AI regulation in the absence of federal action.
  • Existing consumer protection laws are being applied to AI therapy apps.
  • Transparency and data privacy are the central focus of current state-level probes.
