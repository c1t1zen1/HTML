Agentic AI Systems Nearly Double Explained Stock Returns in New Real-Time Earnings Benchmark

A real-time earnings benchmark reports that optimized agentic AI systems can more than double explained stock-return variation, while exposing the limits of prediction claims.

August 22, 2026 · 1:03 AM PDT · 6 min read · 12 sources · Original family: AI Stock Prediction & Market Analysis
agentic AIasset pricingearningsbenchmarks
Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The central finding is that optimized agentic AI systems may substantially improve explanations of stock returns around earnings announcements. The supplied research evidence describes a real-time, out-of-sample benchmark that restricts systems to information available when an announcement occurs, including the announcement text itself. This design directly targets look-ahead bias, a major concern in financial prediction research.

The evidence comes primarily from the benchmark paper and related institutional descriptions, supplemented by a research summary and an independent report. Several sources repeat a result in which explained variation rises from roughly 8% to close to 20% for the best-optimized systems. Another source reports approximately 17% explained same-day stock moves, up from 5%, and cautions that part of the improvement may reflect models learning to predict the moves. The supplied materials do not establish whether these figures use identical samples, metrics, or specifications.

Assignment angle: Unlike generic backtests, this benchmark tests information available at each earnings announcement and measures whether optimized agents improve interpretable, out-of-sample explanations of stock returns without look-ahead bias.

FIELD NOTE

A harder test than a conventional backtest

The benchmark evaluates how well AI systems explain contemporaneous stock returns around earnings announcements using information available at announcement time. That information includes the announcement text itself, according to the benchmark descriptions from Chicago Booth, NBER, and the research paper’s PDF.

This timing constraint matters because it separates information that could have been known at the event from information revealed later. The benchmark is described as real-time and out-of-sample, and the Explaining Markets project characterizes it as an open benchmark for evaluating systems against a real-time standard.

The supplied evidence therefore supports a narrower claim than broad market-beating performance: these systems are being assessed on their ability to explain announcement-related returns under a defined information set.

  • The test focuses on stock returns around earnings announcements.
  • Systems are limited to information available at announcement time.
  • The benchmark is described as real-time and out-of-sample.
  • The evidence does not establish performance across all market conditions or horizons.

FIELD NOTE

Reported improvement in explained variation

The benchmark paper and several related sources report that the best-optimized agentic systems more than double explained variation relative to standard benchmarks, with R2 increasing from 8% to close to 20%. Sources 1, 2, and 7 present this as the headline quantitative result.

A separate Phys.org account reports that the best AI models explained about 17% of same-day stock moves, compared with 5% previously. It also says that the performance increase was partly attributable to models learning to predict the moves. That wording introduces an important distinction between explanation and prediction.

Because the supplied sources do not provide complete methodological details for reconciling the 20% and 17% figures, they should not be treated as interchangeable. The robust conclusion is directional: optimized systems reportedly improve on standard benchmarks, but the exact magnitude depends on the reported specification.

  • Several sources report R2 rising from 8% to close to 20%.
  • Another source reports approximately 17% explained same-day stock moves, up from 5%.
  • The supplied evidence does not show that the two figures use identical definitions.
  • The reported improvement concerns explained variation, not necessarily trading profits.

FIELD NOTE

What optimization adds to agentic systems

The described systems do more than apply a generic language model to earnings text. The benchmark evidence says they extract structured signals from earnings call transcripts and optimize over those signals. This provides a mechanism for converting qualitative disclosures into variables that can be evaluated against contemporaneous returns.

The result, if replicated, suggests that system design and optimization may matter as much as the underlying language model. The benchmark is presented as covering a range of agentic AI systems, while only the best-optimized systems are credited with the largest improvement.

The evidence does not identify one universally superior architecture, nor does it establish that optimization eliminates model error. It indicates that structured signal extraction and optimization can improve benchmark performance under the stated information constraints.

  • Agentic systems extract structured signals from earnings call transcripts.
  • Optimization is performed over those signals.
  • The largest reported gains are associated with the best-optimized systems.
  • The evidence does not identify a single winning model or architecture.

FIELD NOTE

Interpretation limits and open questions

Explaining a larger share of announcement-day returns is not equivalent to forecasting every stock move, generating profitable trades, or identifying causal economic mechanisms. The Phys.org summary specifically notes that some of the performance boost may come from models learning to predict the moves, reinforcing the need to distinguish predictive fit from explanation.

The benchmark’s scope is also specific: earnings announcements and contemporaneous stock returns. A separate arXiv source describes a different daily Russell 1000 nowcasting framework that begins in April 2025 and is designed to be out-of-sample and free of look-ahead bias. That study should not be merged with the earnings-announcement benchmark without additional evidence.

Future evaluation should clarify how the reported R2 figures are calculated, how stable they are across samples, and whether structured signals remain useful after costs, changing disclosure practices, and other real-world constraints. None of those outcomes is established by the supplied snippets.

  • Higher explained variation does not by itself prove causal understanding.
  • The benchmark result does not establish trading profitability.
  • The earnings-event scope differs from daily stock nowcasting.
  • The reported figures require methodological context before direct comparison.

Closing read

Conclusion

The supplied evidence supports a meaningful but bounded result: optimized agentic AI systems reportedly explain substantially more variation in stock returns around earnings announcements than standard benchmarks, under a real-time, out-of-sample information constraint. The repeated estimate of an increase from 8% to close to 20% is the clearest quantitative claim in the materials, while the separate 17% versus 5% account signals that reported performance figures may vary by specification.

The forward-looking implication is a forecast, not an established fact: if the benchmark design and results replicate across additional announcements and independent implementations, structured extraction and optimization could become a stronger framework for analyzing earnings-related market reactions. The evidence does not yet show that these systems deliver causal explanations, durable trading profits, or general market forecasting ability.

Five takeaways

  • Sources 3, 5, and 11 describe a real-time, out-of-sample benchmark restricted to information available at announcement time, including announcement text.
  • Sources 1, 2, and 7 report that the best-optimized systems increase explained variation from 8% to close to 20%.
  • Source 8 reports a different comparison—about 17% of same-day stock moves explained versus 5%—and says some improvement may reflect learned prediction.
  • Sources 1, 2, and 7 describe systems that extract structured signals from earnings call transcripts and optimize over those signals.
  • Source 12 describes a separate daily Russell 1000 nowcasting study, so its no-look-ahead design should not be treated as evidence for the earnings-announcement benchmark itself.
