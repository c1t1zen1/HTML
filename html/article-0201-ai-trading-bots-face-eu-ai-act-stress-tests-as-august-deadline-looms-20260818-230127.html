The August 2026 Deadline: Two Speeds, One Enforcement Date

The EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) entered into force on August 1, 2024, but the substantial applicability date for high-risk AI system obligations is August 2, 2026. According to technology.org, this date sat in compliance calendars for two years as the moment the Act becomes 'substantially applicable.' A compliance tracker at compliancestack.ai describes it as a '0-day countdown to Aug 2026,' with enforcement signals updated weekly. The informedclearly.com analysis confirms the deadline and adds that fines reach up to €35 million or 7% of global turnover. Sources agree that this is not a soft launch: niteagent.com notes that 'nobody is treating 2026 as a soft launch,' and defenderbot.tech explicitly ties full enforcement to August 2, 2026.

Classification First: Not Every Trading Bot Is High-Risk

A central compliance question is whether an AI trading bot qualifies as 'high-risk' under the EU AI Act. Niteagent.com stresses that 'a personal trading assistant or an on-chain execution agent is not automatically high-risk.' Financialregulations.eu clarifies that credit scoring, insurance pricing, and AML screening are explicitly covered as high-risk uses in financial services, but it does not list generic trading bots as automatically high-risk. However, the same guide notes that the AI Act's obligations interact with DORA and MiFID II, meaning that even non-high-risk bots face overlapping regulatory duties. The classification decision is therefore the first step: it determines whether the full stress-testing and bias-audit regime applies or whether lighter transparency obligations suffice.

  • Personal trading assistants and on-chain execution agents are not automatically high-risk per niteagent.com.
  • Credit scoring, insurance pricing, and AML screening are explicitly high-risk uses under the EU AI Act.
  • The AI Act overlaps with DORA and MiFID II, creating layered obligations for trading bots.

Brokers and Algorithmic Trading: MiFID II and RTS 6 Obligations

Even before the AI Act's high-risk classification, brokers and firms running automated strategies already face obligations under MiFID II Article 17 and RTS 6. Wxtrade.com states that 'regulators treat AI trading bots as a form of algorithmic trading rather than a novelty—the obligations attach to the automated activity, not the AI label.' This means firms must notify regulators, test systems, and keep detailed order records. Key2law.com similarly reports that AI trading bots in forex must follow new compliance rules every broker must know in 2026. These existing duties complement the AI Act, creating a stacked compliance regime. The August 2026 date intensifies the pressure because AI Act high-risk obligations add stress-testing and bias-audit layers on top of MiFID II's testing and record-keeping.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence confirms that August 2, 2026, is the enforcement date for the EU AI Act's high-risk obligations, and that AI trading bots face a layered compliance regime combining the AI Act with MiFID II and RTS 6. The classification of a bot as high-risk is not automatic—personal assistants and on-chain agents may fall outside the high-risk category, but credit scoring and similar financial uses are explicitly covered. Formal stress-testing and bias audits are central to high-risk obligations, while all automated trading activity already carries notification and testing duties under MiFID II. The performance conversation is expanding to include compliance, safety, and survival, as reflected in rankings and guides.

  • High-risk bots face formal stress-testing and bias audits, while all automated trading faces MiFID II Article 17 and RTS 6 notification/testing duties.
