Congress Probes Intelligence Agencies’ AI Searches of Americans’ Commercial Data

Congress is probing how intelligence agencies use AI to search Americans' commercially purchased data, as oversight bills and FISA renewal collide.

Why this matters now

This article examines congressional oversight of AI-enabled querying of commercially purchased personal data—a distinct issue from border-drone surveillance or election-focused deepfakes. The evidence draws from EPIC's surveillance oversight program, Brennan Center analysis of Pentagon AI deals, California Attorney General Rob Bonta's coalition letter, House intelligence authorization language, and reporting on FISA Section 702 renewal. FIELD NOTE

The Commercial Data Loophole

Intelligence agencies can purchase commercially available information from data brokers without a warrant, and AI tools now allow them to analyze that data at scale. EPIC's Jeramie D. Scott warns that the government wants to use AI to analyze Americans' information obtained without a warrant through data broker purchases and 'incidental' collection from foreign intelligence surveillance. The Brennan Center for Justice notes that in May 2026 the Pentagon announced deals with eight AI companies—SpaceX, OpenAI, Google, NVIDIA, Reflection, Microsoft, Amazon Web Services, and Oracle—to deploy advanced AI capabilities on Department systems. Just Security cautions that access to commercial large language models gives the Pentagon a powerful surveillance tool that could be turned on Americans.

  • EPIC warns AI is being used to analyze warrantless data broker purchases and incidental FISA collection.
  • Pentagon announced deals with eight AI companies in May 2026 to deploy advanced AI capabilities.
  • Commercial LLMs could be turned into a surveillance tool on Americans, according to Just Security.

FIELD NOTE

Congressional Oversight Measures

Lawmakers are moving to tighten oversight of commercial data purchases. The House passed the Fiscal Year 2026 Intelligence Authorization Act (IAA) with language signaling that the Intelligence Community's purchases of commercially available information from data brokers will no longer escape scrutiny. The House intel bill would establish an IC chief AI officer, illuminate how agencies fund open-source intelligence, and give the Intelligence Community Inspector General new authorities. Separately, ten bipartisan AI bills advanced in a single markup session in the House Science, Space, and Technology Committee, covering research access, cybersecurity, workforce development, transparency, and data center energy standards. The Great American AI Act of 2026 discussion draft has support from Reps. Suhas Subramanyam, Scott Franklin, Scott Peters, and Erin Houchin.

  • House IAA includes language tightening oversight of commercial data purchases.
  • House intel bill would create an IC chief AI officer and expand IG authorities.
  • Ten bipartisan AI bills advanced in the House Science, Space, and Technology Committee.
  • Great American AI Act discussion draft backed by four representatives.

FIELD NOTE

FISA Section 702 and the Renewal Fight

The renewal of FISA Section 702 is a flashpoint for the commercial data issue. Congress passed a 45-day extension of the surveillance law, which allows warrantless searches of data scooped up by America's intelligence agencies. Privacy advocates warn that AI could allow government agencies to analyze Americans' communications and personal data on a scale that was not possible when the law was enacted. EPIC calls on Congress to demand closures to loopholes before any renewal of Section 702. The incidental collection from foreign intelligence surveillance can include Americans' data, making AI-enabled querying a central concern in the reauthorization debate.

  • Congress passed a 45-day extension of the surveillance law.
  • Privacy advocates warn AI could enable unprecedented analysis of Americans' communications and personal data.
  • EPIC urges Congress to act before Section 702 renewal.

State Attorneys General and the Push for Judicial Oversight

California Attorney General Rob Bonta, joined by a coalition of 17 attorneys general, called on Congress to take immediate action to halt federal agencies' use of commercially purchased data and artificial intelligence tools that enable mass surveillance of Americans without judicial, legislative, or public oversight. The coalition's letter urges Congress to address the loophole, adding state-level pressure alongside federal oversight efforts. This push highlights the absence of a warrant requirement for AI-enabled searches of commercial data.

  • Bonta and 17 attorneys general called for a halt to commercial data and AI mass surveillance.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence shows Congress is actively probing AI-enabled searches of commercially purchased data, but the outcome remains uncertain. Fact: the House passed an Intelligence Authorization Act with oversight language, and a 45-day FISA extension was enacted. Fact: the Pentagon announced AI deals with eight companies, and 17 attorneys general sent a letter urging action. Projection: privacy advocates warn that AI could enable mass surveillance of Americans' data, but the actual scale of current agency use is not confirmed in the sources. The renewal of Section 702 will be a decisive moment—whether Congress closes the commercial data loophole or preserves warrantless AI querying. The oversight bills and state pressure suggest momentum for transparency, but the 45-day extension indicates that surveillance authorities remain in force.
