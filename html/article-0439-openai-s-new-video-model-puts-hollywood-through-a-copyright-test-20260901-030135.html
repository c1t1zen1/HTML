OpenAI's New Video Model Puts Hollywood Through a Copyright Test

OpenAI's Sora 2 video model triggers immediate copyright lawsuits from Hollywood studios, testing fair use defenses and training-data transparency.

Why this matters now

OpenAI's recently released Sora 2 text-to-video model is facing immediate copyright litigation and regulatory pressure from major studios, creating a live test of how generative AI video models handle copyrighted training data and fair use defenses. Evidence was gathered from twelve sources including BizTech Weekly, Reddit r/technology, TechBuzz AI, Copyright Lately, CineD, The New York Times, Los Angeles Times, The Hollywood Reporter, Variety, Yahoo News, and the Motion Picture Association, covering the period from the model's launch through ongoing legal and policy developments. FIELD NOTE

Hollywood's Copyright Challenge to Sora 2

Major entertainment industry groups have moved quickly to challenge OpenAI's Sora 2 video generation model on copyright grounds. The Motion Picture Association has demanded that OpenAI crack down on copyright violations flooding the platform, as users create unauthorized AI clips featuring characters such as James Bond and Mario. Hollywood argues that OpenAI cannot use copyrighted content without explicit permission or compensation, and that the opt-out process escalates what it views as a cavalier approach to intellectual property rights. The entertainment industry has reached what analysts describe as a turning point, with the erosion of traditional copyright protections becoming a central concern as generative video models produce realistic likenesses of protected characters and properties. The materials upon which OpenAI teaches its system remain a black box, with the company stating only that Sora 2 is trained on diverse datasets including information accessed through third-party partnerships and publicly available internet content. This opacity has fueled suspicion among rightsholders who fear their intellectual property was used without consent to build competitive generative capabilities.

Legal Backlash and Litigation

Sora 2 has attracted multiple legal challenges since its release, with lawsuits accumulating alongside existing litigation against OpenAI. Beyond the Motion Picture Association's warnings, Cameo has filed a copyright infringement lawsuit against the model, and industry observers note that Disney and Universal are already suing other AI companies such as Midjourney for allowing generation of images that blatantly copy protected characters. Reddit commentary reflects frustration that OpenAI faces so many lawsuits simultaneously, with critics suggesting the company wants users generating videos to harvest synthetic data for future model iterations. The legal questions center on whether training on copyrighted internet content constitutes fair use, whether opt-out systems adequately protect rightsholders, and whether revenue-sharing proposals can resolve disputes without litigation. Because these cases are ongoing, the outcomes remain uncertain and should be treated as contested rather than settled law.

  • Cameo filed a copyright infringement lawsuit against Sora 2
  • Disney and Universal are suing Midjourney over character copying

Policy Reversal and Opt-Out Controversy

Facing mounting backlash, OpenAI has reversed course on its copyright policy for Sora 2, announcing that rightsholders will decide whether their copyrighted characters can be generated by the video app. The new approach includes revenue sharing for those who opt in, shifting from an opt-out model that critics argued forced Hollywood to police unauthorized uses. However, the opt-out process itself has been criticized as escalating OpenAI's cavalier approach to intellectual property rights, with some analysts arguing that rightsholders should not bear the burden of preventing misuse of their own intellectual property. The policy change may prevent some immediate lawsuits, but it also confirms that the entertainment industry has reached a turning point in how it engages with generative AI technologies. The effectiveness of the opt-in system remains to be seen, as does whether revenue-sharing terms will satisfy major studios accustomed to controlling character usage.

  • OpenAI reversed course to let rightsholders decide character generation
  • Revenue sharing introduced for those who opt in to Sora 2
  • Opt-out process criticized as placing burden on rightsholders
