Matter 1.5 Standardization Sparks 2026 Smart Home Interoperability Wars

Matter 1.5, released in late 2025, expands smart home interoperability to cameras and energy management, setting the stage for 2026 hardware competition and the CSA's Unify event.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The Connectivity Standards Alliance (CSA) released Matter 1.5 on November 20, 2025, marking a significant expansion of the smart home interoperability standard. This update introduces support for high-impact device categories previously excluded, specifically cameras, video doorbells, and unified closures, alongside enhanced energy management capabilities and soil sensors. The release aims to complete the standard's coverage of major device categories, theoretically enabling a whole-home ecosystem where devices from different manufacturers can communicate seamlessly.

Following the protocol release, the industry has moved into a phase of hardware implementation and competitive positioning. By early 2026, consumer hardware supporting these new features is beginning to reach the market, coinciding with the CSA's inaugural Unify industry event scheduled for June 2026. An incremental update, Matter 1.5.1, has also been announced to refine camera performance and device flexibility, indicating rapid iteration in response to early adoption challenges and manufacturer feedback.

FIELD NOTE

Protocol Expansion: Cameras, Closures, and Energy

Matter 1.5 represents a substantial shift from previous iterations by addressing security and utility monitoring. The standard now explicitly supports cameras and video doorbells, a category that had been a notable gap in the interoperability landscape. This addition allows for cross-platform video streaming and control, breaking down silos between security ecosystems.

Simultaneously, the update introduces support for unified closures, which encompasses door and window sensors, as well as soil sensors for garden monitoring. These additions extend the standard's reach into physical security and environmental monitoring. Furthermore, Matter 1.5 advances energy management capabilities, providing a broader set of energy data features that allow devices to report and manage power usage more effectively within a unified framework.

  • Adds support for cameras and video doorbells.
  • Introduces unified closures (doors/windows) and soil sensors.
  • Expands energy management data features.

Refinement and Incremental Updates

Following the initial release of Matter 1.5, the CSA has already issued Matter 1.5.1, an incremental update focused on enhancing camera performance. This rapid follow-up indicates that the initial implementation of camera support required adjustments to ensure high-quality product development and user experience. The 1.5.1 update aims to make it easier for device makers to build reliable camera and doorbell products while allowing ecosystems to deliver more efficient and flexible experiences.

This iterative approach suggests that while the foundational protocol is set, the practical execution of complex features like video streaming requires ongoing refinement. The existence of 1.5.1 highlights the dynamic nature of the standard, where feedback from the first wave of hardware developers directly influences subsequent protocol tweaks.

Competitive Dynamics and Market Positioning

The introduction of Matter 1.5 has intensified the 'interoperability wars' by raising the bar for smart home devices. With support for cameras and energy management, the standard now covers high-value, high-traffic categories that were previously locked into proprietary ecosystems. This forces major players to either adopt the standard to remain competitive or risk being isolated from the emerging unified smart home market.

The presence of established brands like Nanoleaf, Philips Hue, Eve, and Aqara in the marketplace with Matter-enabled products demonstrates that the ecosystem is already active. However, the addition of cameras and energy features may shift competitive dynamics, as these categories often drive consumer purchase decisions. The 2026 period is characterized by a race to implement these new features in consumer-facing hardware before the Unify event and subsequent holiday seasons.
