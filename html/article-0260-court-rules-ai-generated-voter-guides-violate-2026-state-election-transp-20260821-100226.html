State courts are striking down AI deepfake laws on First Amendment grounds, creating a patchwork of election transparency rules that complicates compliance for campaigns in 2026.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The legal landscape for AI-generated voter information in the 2026 election cycle is defined by a significant divergence between legislative intent and judicial enforcement. While multiple states have enacted laws requiring disclosure or banning AI deepfakes in elections, recent court rulings have invalidated specific provisions in California and Hawaii on First Amendment grounds. This creates a complex environment where campaigns must navigate a fragmented set of state-specific regulations rather than a uniform federal standard.

The evidence indicates that while the Federal Election Commission (FEC) has issued interpretive rules regarding AI in campaign ads, state-level enforcement remains the primary battleground. Legislatures in states like Massachusetts are continuing to pass bills aimed at preventing the manipulation of voters, even as other states adjust their approaches following legal setbacks. The current situation reflects a tension between the desire for election integrity and constitutional protections for political speech.
