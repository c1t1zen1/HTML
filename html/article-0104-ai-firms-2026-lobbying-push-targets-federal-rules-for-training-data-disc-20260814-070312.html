The Federal Disclosure Landscape

At the federal level, the AI Foundation Model Transparency Act of 2026 (H.R.8094) has been introduced in the 119th Congress, signaling legislative interest in requiring transparency from foundation models. The bill's short title suggests it would mandate disclosure of training data and other model characteristics, though the full text is not yet public in the available evidence. Meanwhile, state legislation is already moving on training-data transparency: more than 35 states have active AI bills covering areas including training-data transparency and privacy, according to a March 2026 legislative update. This patchwork of state rules creates pressure for a uniform federal standard, which is exactly the kind of rule AI firms might seek to influence.

  • H.R.8094, the AI Foundation Model Transparency Act of 2026, is pending in Congress.
  • State-level AI legislation in over 35 states includes training-data transparency and privacy provisions.
  • Federal rules on training-data disclosure are not yet enacted, leaving room for lobbying.

Record Lobbying Spending by OpenAI and Anthropic

OpenAI and Anthropic increased their federal lobbying spending to record levels in the second quarter of 2026, according to CNBC. TechBuzz reports the two firms spent a combined $3.17 million on lobbying during Q2 2026, a 23% jump from the previous quarter. These figures come from federal disclosure filings, which are required quarterly under the Lobbying Disclosure Act. The spending surge coincides with a period of intense AI policy debate in Washington, including discussions about transparency, copyright, and safety. However, the public filings do not break out how much of that spending is directed specifically at training-data disclosure rules.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence confirms that AI firms are spending record amounts on federal lobbying and that training-data and copyright are among the issues lobbyists are bundling. However, the public record does not yet show whether those efforts are specifically aimed at shaping federal disclosure requirements for copyrighted training data. The AI Foundation Model Transparency Act exists, but its fate and the industry's stance remain unclear. The state-level push for training-data transparency suggests a broader trend, but federal action is still pending. It is a forecast, not a fact, that AI companies will use their lobbying firepower to influence whatever federal disclosure rule emerges—likely favoring industry-friendly flexibility over rigid mandates.
