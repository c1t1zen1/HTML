Qwen3 Quantized Models Turn Mac Unified Memory Into a Local AI Testbed

Qwen3 quantization plus MLX turns Mac unified memory into a practical local-AI testbed—MoE 35B fits in 19.5GB and beats 27B on M1 Max.

Why this matters now

This article synthesizes recent developer benchmarks that treat Mac unified memory as a quantized-model testbed, not just a headline-speed showcase. FIELD NOTE

Quantized Qwen3 MoE models redefine the memory ceiling

Source 1 reports that Qwen 3.5 35B-A3B MLX 4-bit peaks at roughly 19.5GB of unified memory during inference, with weights at 19.5GB and a KV cache adding 1-3GB depending on context. Source 2, measured on an M1 Max 64GB, names Qwen3.6-35B-A3B in 4-bit as a top pick and notes that the MoE 35B ran 3.7x faster than the 27B dense model. Source 5 frames memory-tier decisions for 64GB/128GB/192GB Macs and highlights Q4/Q8 trade-offs, while source 7 lists memory footprints across M1 through M5 Max. Together these benchmarks show that quantization collapses a 35B-class MoE into the memory budget of a 24GB or 64GB Mac, making local inference practical without cloud offload.

  • Qwen3.6-35B-A3B in 4-bit is a top pick on M1 Max 64GB and ran 3.7x faster than the 27B dense model.

FIELD NOTE

MLX deployment changes the speed baseline on Apple Silicon

Source 8 states that MLX, Apple's native ML framework, beats llama.cpp by 30-40% on M5 hardware. Source 9 gives a concrete example: on a Mac mini M4 Pro (64GB) running Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B, MLX achieved approximately 130 tok/s versus 43 tok/s for Ollama with llama.cpp. Source 10, from Apple's ML research, benchmarks Qwen 1.7B, 8B, and 4-bit quantized 8B/14B on M5 and M4 using MLX. Source 12 notes that MLX targets researchers and developers with a NumPy-like API, while llama.cpp offers granular control over offloading and quantization. The practical effect is that MLX becomes the default for Qwen3 quantized models on Macs, shifting the bottleneck from framework choice to memory bandwidth.

  • MLX beats llama.cpp by 30-40% on M5 hardware.
  • On M4 Pro 64GB, Qwen3-Coder-30B-A3B hit ~130 tok/s with MLX vs 43 tok/s with Ollama/llama.cpp.
  • Apple's ML research evaluated Qwen 1.7B, 8B, and 4-bit 8B/14B on M5 and M4 with MLX.

FIELD NOTE

Hardware tiers: from M1 Max to M5 Max

Source 2 provides measured numbers on an M1 Max 64GB with mlx-lm 0.31.3, showing Qwen3.6-35B-A3B in 4-bit is viable but not necessarily fast. Source 3 reports M5 Max delivers about 28% higher tok/s than M4 Max, with 600GB/s bandwidth and 128GB unified memory. Source 6 offers a reproducible benchmark harness for Qwen3.5-9B on M4 Max. Source 11's index, as of July 2026, lists Gemma 4 E2B at roughly 158 tok/s on M5 Max via MLX, illustrating the ceiling. The tiered picture: a 64GB M1 Max can run 35B MoE, but M5 Max with 128GB and higher bandwidth becomes the practical testbed for larger quantized Qwen3 variants.

Quantization trade-offs and swap risk

Source 5 frames Q4/Q8 trade-offs, swap risk, and when to offload heavy inference to a remote Mac node. Source 10 evaluated 4-bit quantized Qwen 8B and 14B on M5, showing quantization is a practical path but with quality implications. Source 12 warns that quantization parameters matter and that building llama.cpp directly gives granular control. The developer takeaway: Q4 saves memory and fits more models, but Q8 or BF16 may be needed for quality-sensitive work; exceeding unified memory triggers swap, which destroys token-generation latency.

  • Q4/Q8 trade-offs and swap risk are central to choosing a Mac memory tier.
  • Apple's M5 research tested 4-bit quantized Qwen 8B and 14B.
  • llama.cpp allows granular control over quantization parameters, while MLX offers a simpler API.

Closing read

Conclusion

The measured evidence shows that Qwen3 quantization plus MLX deployment has moved Mac unified memory from a curiosity to a practical local-inference testbed. A 35B-A3B MoE model fits in roughly 19.5GB of weights, runs on a 64GB M1 Max, and delivers 3.7x faster token generation than a 27B dense model. MLX consistently outperforms llama.cpp on Apple Silicon, with a 30-40% advantage on M5 and a specific 130 vs 43 tok/s result on M4 Pro. Hardware tiers matter: M5 Max adds 28% throughput and 600GB/s bandwidth, making it the likely reference platform for larger quantized Qwen3 variants. These are measured facts from the cited sources. Projections, clearly labeled as forecasts, include: future Qwen3 releases will likely follow the same quantization pattern, MLX will remain the default framework for Mac local inference, and 24GB Macs may become entry-level testbeds for 35B MoE models. Developers should monitor model-naming changes and memory-footprint reports to keep benchmarks reproducible.

  • M5 Max delivers ~28% higher tok/s than M4 Max with 600GB/s bandwidth and 128GB unified memory (source 3).
