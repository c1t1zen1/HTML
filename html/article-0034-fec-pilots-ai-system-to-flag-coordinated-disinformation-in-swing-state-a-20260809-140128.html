FEC Pilots AI System to Flag Coordinated Disinformation in Swing State Ads

The FEC tests an AI tool to spot coordinated disinformation in swing state political ads, marking its first proactive use of generative AI detection.

August 9, 2026 · 2:01 PM PDT · 4 min read · 12 sources · Original family: AI in US Elections & Campaigns
AIElection SecurityDisinformationFEC
Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The Federal Election Commission is piloting an AI system designed to identify coordinated inauthentic behavior across political ad buys in key battleground states. This initiative represents the agency's first proactive deployment of generative AI to detect synthetic media networks in election advertising. The move comes amid growing concerns over AI-generated political content and the absence of federal regulation governing deepfakes in campaigns.

FIELD NOTE

FEC's AI Pilot Targets Coordinated Ad Networks

The Federal Election Commission is testing an artificial intelligence system to detect coordinated inauthentic behavior in political advertisements, particularly in swing states. The tool is designed to identify patterns suggesting synthetic media networks operating across multiple ad buys. This marks the FEC's first known use of generative AI for proactive monitoring of election-related content. The pilot focuses on uncovering disinformation campaigns that may exploit AI-generated media to influence voter perceptions.

Regulatory Response to AI-Generated Political Ads

Lawmakers at both state and federal levels are responding to the surge in AI-generated political content with new legislative proposals. The FEC has launched hearings to explore rules governing AI-generated campaign materials, reflecting heightened regulatory scrutiny. Meanwhile, a growing number of states have enacted laws addressing deepfakes in political communication, though approaches vary widely. These developments underscore the urgency of addressing synthetic media in elections amid federal regulatory inertia.

Prevalence and Risks of AI-Generated Content in 2026 Campaigns

AI-generated political ads now account for approximately 30% of all political advertising in the 2026 election cycle. Deepfake political ads are actively circulating in multiple states, with documented impacts on specific races such as those in Georgia and New York. Despite the proliferation of synthetic media, the FEC remains constrained by a 3-3 partisan deadlock, preventing the adoption of federal rules. This regulatory gap has left voters exposed to deceptive content without clear federal safeguards.

Closing read

Conclusion

The FEC's pilot of an AI system to detect coordinated disinformation in political ads marks a significant step toward addressing synthetic media threats in elections. Evidence shows that AI-generated content now constitutes a substantial share of political advertising and has already influenced specific races, while state-level responses remain fragmented and insufficient without federal action. The pilot reflects growing recognition within the commission of the need for proactive tools to monitor inauthentic behavior, even as broader regulatory efforts remain stalled. However, the success of this initiative depends on the tool's accuracy, scalability, and ability to operate within the FEC's existing authority and resource constraints. Without complementary rulemaking or congressional action, the pilot may remain a limited experiment rather than a foundation for systemic change.
