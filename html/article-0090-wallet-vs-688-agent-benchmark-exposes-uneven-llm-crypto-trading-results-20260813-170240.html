Wallet V’s 688-Agent Benchmark Exposes Uneven LLM Crypto Trading Results

Wallet V's 688-agent benchmark shows AI trading bots on Hyperliquid/Aster are uneven: only 42% profitable, peak ROI +307%.

August 13, 2026 · 5:02 PM PDT · 5 min read · 12 sources · Original family: AI Crypto Trading Bots & Performance

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

Wallet V, a self-custody Web3 wallet incubated by Virgo Group, released a public performance benchmark for AI trading agents that its users configured on the decentralized derivatives platforms Hyperliquid and Aster. The benchmark, hosted on Wallet V's website, aggregates the on-platform results of 688 agents created over the prior two months. Each agent was configured by the user, used a large language model selected by the user to generate trading decisions, and executed on Hyperliquid or Aster. The reported figures show that only 42% of agents finished with positive returns, while the peak ROI reached +307%, revealing a wide dispersion in outcomes. This article examines what the benchmark shows, why the dispersion matters, and what it implies for AI-driven crypto trading.

FIELD NOTE

A Public Benchmark for User-Configured AI Agents

Wallet V, a self-custody Web3 wallet, launched a public performance benchmark for AI trading agents on June 15, 2026. The benchmark covers 688 agents created by Wallet V users over the prior two months. Each agent was configured by the user, used a large language model selected by the user to generate trading decisions, and executed on Hyperliquid or Aster, both decentralized derivatives platforms. Wallet V aggregates the on-platform performance of those agents by underlying model, making it one of the first public cross-model comparisons of real user-configured AI trading bots.

  • Benchmark covers 688 agents created by Wallet V users over two months.
  • Agents used user-selected large language models and executed on Hyperliquid or Aster.
  • Wallet V aggregates performance by underlying model on its website.

The Numbers: 42% Profitable, Peak ROI +307%

The benchmark's headline figures are stark: only 42% of the 688 agents finished with positive returns, while the peak ROI reached +307%. This means the majority of AI-configured trading agents lost money during the measured period. The wide gap between the top performer and the rest underscores the unevenness of results across different models and user configurations. The benchmark does not yet provide a full distribution of returns, but the reported figures already suggest that selecting a profitable AI trading agent is far from guaranteed.

  • 42% of agents finished positive.
  • Peak ROI reached +307%.
  • Majority of agents did not achieve profitability.

Why Dispersion Matters for AI Trading

The dispersion in results is significant because it shows that the choice of LLM and user configuration can lead to dramatically different outcomes. Since Wallet V aggregates performance by underlying model, the benchmark is designed to expose which models tend to perform better or worse in live trading conditions. However, the sources do not yet reveal specific model rankings or the distribution of returns. The fact that only 42% of agents were profitable suggests that many LLM-driven trading strategies are not consistently reliable, and that performance may depend heavily on prompt design, risk parameters, and market conditions.

  • Performance varies widely across user-configured agents.
  • Aggregation by model allows cross-model comparison.
  • No specific model rankings were disclosed in the initial announcement.

Implications for Traders and Developers

For traders, the benchmark provides a rare public glimpse into real-world AI trading performance, but it also carries a caution: past performance is not indicative of future results, and the 42% profitability rate means most agents lose money. For developers, the data could help refine LLM prompts and trading logic, but the lack of full distribution details limits deeper analysis. The benchmark is hosted on Wallet V's website and is expected to be updated, but the initial release is a snapshot of a two-month window, not a long-term track record.

  • Traders should treat the benchmark as a transparency tool, not a guarantee.

  • Users may stop unprofitable agents, creating selection bias in the benchmark cohort.
  • LLM-generated trading decisions can be non-deterministic, making reproducibility difficult.

Closing read

Conclusion

Wallet V's public benchmark of 688 AI trading agents on Hyperliquid and Aster is a meaningful step toward transparency in AI-driven crypto trading. The reported figures — 42% profitability and a peak ROI of +307% — confirm that results are highly uneven and that most user-configured agents did not make money. The benchmark aggregates performance by underlying model, which could eventually reveal which LLMs and configurations are more reliable, but the initial release does not provide enough data to draw definitive conclusions. As the benchmark grows and is updated, it may become a useful reference, but for now it serves as a reminder that AI trading bots are not a sure path to profits.
