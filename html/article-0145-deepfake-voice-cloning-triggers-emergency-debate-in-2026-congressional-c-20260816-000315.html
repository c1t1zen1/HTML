Federal Legislative Momentum: NO FAKES and DEFIANCE Acts

Congressional action in 2026 has coalesced around two primary federal bills targeting synthetic media. The NO FAKES Act, which bans AI-powered voice cloning and deepfakes without consent, has advanced through the Senate Judiciary Committee. This bill establishes a federal right to sue for unauthorized cloning, marking a significant procedural shift in how victims can seek redress.

Complementing this is the DEFIANCE Act, which the Senate passed in January 2026. This legislation provides deepfake victims with the ability to claim up to $250,000 in civil damages. Together, these bills represent a dual approach: one focusing on the right to sue for consent violations, and the other establishing specific financial penalties for harm caused by synthetic content.

  • DEFIANCE Act passed by Senate in January 2026.

State-Level Frameworks and the 120-Day Election Window

While federal bills advance, state laws provide the immediate operational constraints for 2026 campaigns. California’s Defending Democracy from Deepfake Deception Act, passed in 2024, serves as a key precedent. It requires platforms to block or label AI-generated political content during the 120-day period leading up to an election. This law also grants candidates a private right of action against creators or distributors of offending content.
