FCC Proposes Real-Time AI Disclosure Mandate for 2026 Digital Ad Buy

The FCC proposes mandatory on-air and written disclosures for AI-generated political ads on radio and television, aiming to ensure voters are informed during the 2026 cycle.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The Federal Communications Commission (FCC) has initiated a proceeding to propose rules requiring the disclosure of AI-generated content in political advertisements on radio and television. This regulatory action follows a proposal by FCC Chairwoman Jessica Rosenworcel in May 2024 and was formalized through a Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (NPRM) in August 2024. The initiative is part of a broader effort to maintain transparency in political advertising as emerging technologies become more prevalent in campaign materials.

The evidence indicates that the proposed rules would require stations airing political ads to inquire about AI usage and inform advertisers of disclosure requirements. While the primary focus of the FCC's current proposal is on broadcast media (radio and TV), the context of the 2026 election cycle highlights a growing intersection with digital advertising standards. The FCC emphasizes that such transparency serves the public interest by fostering an informed electorate, although questions regarding the Commission's authority and the specific scope of regulation remain under public comment.

FIELD NOTE

Regulatory Scope and Proposed Requirements

The FCC's proposed rulemaking specifically targets political advertisements on radio and television. The core requirement is the on-air and written disclosure of AI-generated content. Under the proposal, stations would be required to inquire with the party requesting airtime whether the ad contains AI-generated content. If it does, the station must inform the advertiser that an on-air disclosure is required.

The Commission defines the scope to ensure consumers are fully informed when AI technology is used. The proposal was initiated to address the unique threats AI poses to democratic processes by ensuring transparency. The FCC has launched a proceeding to take public comment on these standards, indicating that the rules are not yet final and are subject to revision based on feedback.

  • Requires on-air and written disclosure for AI content in radio/TV political ads.
  • Stations must inquire about AI usage before airing political ads.
  • Stations must inform advertisers of the disclosure requirement.
  • Proceeding is open for public comment to refine the rules.

Timeline and Procedural Status

Intersection with Digital and State Standards

While the FCC's specific proposal focuses on radio and television, the broader landscape of 2026 political advertising compliance includes digital platforms. For instance, Washington state’s Public Disclosure Commission requires sponsor identification for political ads on digital platforms, including those appearing on commercial advertisers. This suggests a multi-layered regulatory environment where federal broadcast rules may coexist with state-level digital disclosure mandates.

Additionally, other jurisdictions are implementing their own AI-specific disclosure laws. New York, for example, has a law effective June 9, 2026, requiring disclosure when AI-generated synthetic performers are used in advertising. These state-level actions indicate a trend toward stricter transparency standards that may influence or complement the FCC's federal approach, particularly as the 2026 cycle approaches.

Public Interest and Democratic Safeguards

The final rules will likely reflect a compromise between these competing interests.

Conclusion

The FCC's proposal to mandate real-time disclosures for AI-generated political ads on radio and television represents a significant step toward regulating emerging technologies in the 2026 election cycle. The evidence confirms that the Commission has initiated a formal rulemaking process, requiring stations to inquire about AI usage and enforce on-air disclosures. This action is distinct from general content moderation, focusing specifically on transparency regarding the origin of the media content.

However, the scope of the FCC's current proposal is limited to broadcast media, leaving digital platforms to be addressed by state laws or other federal agencies. The final outcome of the rulemaking is not yet determined, as the FCC is still gathering public comment on legal authority and public interest implications. Readers should note that while the FCC's proposal is a concrete regulatory action, the broader landscape of AI disclosure in 2026 will likely involve a patchwork of federal and state regulations, including specific mandates for synthetic media in states like New York.
