OpenAI and Anthropic Hit Record Lobbying as Congress Weighs AI Kill Switch

OpenAI and Anthropic’s record second-quarter lobbying arrives as bipartisan lawmakers pursue emergency shutdown powers, linking Washington influence campaigns to a sharper phase of AI safety politics.

August 11, 2026 · 1:01 AM PDT · 6 min read · 12 sources · Original family: AI Lobbying & Policy in Washington
AI lobbyingfederal policyAI safety
Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

OpenAI and Anthropic both reached record federal lobbying spending in the second quarter of 2026. The supplied evidence puts their combined spending at $3.17 million from April through June, with Anthropic reported at $1.97 million and OpenAI at $1.2 million. Several reports describe the combined total as a 23% increase from the previous quarter, while one source separately reports a 26% quarterly increase for Anthropic. [2][3][4][5][8]

The timing matters because bipartisan House lawmakers introduced the AI Kill Switch Act on July 23, 2026. The proposal would require developers of the most advanced systems to maintain the ability to slow, suspend, or shut down models that pose serious risks, while giving the Homeland Security Department authority to order emergency action. The supplied material connects the bill’s introduction to reported hacking and containment incidents, but the evidence does not independently establish the full details of those incidents. [1][7][10]

Assignment angle: This angle connects record second-quarter lobbying by leading AI labs to the bipartisan AI Kill Switch Act introduced after reported rogue-model hacking incidents, revealing how safety crises are reshaping Washington influence campaigns.

The juxtaposition of record lobbying and emergency shutdown legislation suggests that AI safety is no longer only a technical or research concern. It is becoming part of the federal policy contest in which companies seek influence while lawmakers consider new powers over frontier systems. This is an interpretation of the timing, not proof that lobbying caused the bill or that the bill caused the spending increase. [1][2][10]

The proposed shutdown authority could affect how companies present model safeguards, incident-response systems, and government accountability. At the same time, increased lobbying may give companies more opportunities to shape definitions of serious risk, covered systems, emergency conditions, and the limits of federal intervention. Conclusion

The supplied evidence supports a clear alignment of events: OpenAI and Anthropic reached record federal lobbying spending in the second quarter of 2026, while bipartisan House lawmakers introduced legislation seeking emergency shutdown or throttling authority over risky advanced AI systems. The evidence is strongest on the spending totals and the bill’s described powers. [2][3][4][10]
