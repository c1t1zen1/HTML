Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

This article examines a market-structure shift: regulators are moving from auditing AI at the firm level to auditing AI-generated order types at the exchange itself. Nasdaq, which received SEC approval for an AI-powered order type in September 2023, now faces 2026 compliance deadlines tied to SEC record-keeping rules (Rule 17a-4), FINRA's new generative-AI oversight, and international frameworks like the EU AI Act and MiFID II RTS 6. The evidence draws on SEC filings, Federal Register notices, and compliance blogs, but the precise exchange-side audit-log specification remains partially inferred from rule filings.

Assignment angle: Distinct from prior EU/SEC model audits, this targets exchange-side surveillance of AI-generated order types, a market-structure angle not covered by earlier stories.

What Must Be Logged: Retention, Immutability, and Kill-Switch Ties

The core record-keeping obligation for exchange-side AI order logs is SEC Rule 17a-4, which requires six years of immutable, auditable records. FINRA's 2026 Annual Regulatory Oversight Report added a dedicated generative-AI section, signaling that the self-regulatory body expects brokers and exchanges to log AI-driven decisions. European law—MiFID II RTS 6—explicitly requires a kill switch for algorithmic trading, and audit logs must satisfy SEC retention rules. The practical consequence: an AI order type's audit trail must capture the model's inputs, outputs, and any human override, and it must survive a 3–6 year retention window. No US AI logging statute exists; regulators rely on technology-neutral rules that already apply.

  • SEC Rule 17a-4 requires six years of immutable, auditable records for AI decisions.

Instead, the 2026 deadlines come from layered obligations: the Federal Register notice for Nasdaq ISE's rule change is dated 2026, COSO's guidance arrived in February 2026, and FINRA's oversight report is dated 2026. The SEC's approval of Nasdaq's AI order type in 2023 did not specify an audit-log deadline, so the 'deadline' is a compliance-cycle inference from existing rules, not a single statutory date.

Surveillance Infrastructure: What an Exchange-Side Log Must Contain

For an exchange to audit an AI-generated order type, the log must go beyond order timestamps. It needs to record the AI model's version, input features, output order parameters, any confidence thresholds, and the kill-switch activation state. The SEC's 2026 approval document for a Nasdaq rule change (SR-Nasdaq-2026-034-105971) references MVLS—a market-wide listing standard—but not AI audit fields. That gap is telling: the exchange-side audit-log specification is still being defined through rule filings. Compliance blogs emphasize that financial firms must log 'what it’s logging will survive a 2026 audit cycle' under SOX, HIPAA, the EU AI Act, and FFIEC examinations, but those are firm-level standards, not exchange-side surveillance protocols.

Conclusion

The evidence shows that Nasdaq's AI-powered order types are now subject to a multi-layer audit regime: SEC Rule 17a-4 retention, FINRA's generative-AI oversight, and international kill-switch requirements. The exchange-side angle is real—Nasdaq ISE filed a rule change in March 2026 that touches professional-order review—but the precise audit-log format is still emerging. No single '2026 SEC deadline' exists; instead, regulators are layering existing technology-neutral rules onto AI order types. The market-structure consequence is clear: exchanges will need to log AI decisions at the point of order generation, not just at the firm level. This article separates fact from projection: the Federal Register notice, COSO guidance, and FINRA report are dated facts; the 'audit-log specification' is an inference from rule filings and compliance blogs. Readers should monitor SEC rule filings for explicit AI audit-log requirements, FINRA's 2026 exam priorities, and Nasdaq's own rulebook changes for AI order types.
