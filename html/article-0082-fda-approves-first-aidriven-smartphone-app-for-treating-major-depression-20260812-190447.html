FDA Approves First AI‑Driven Smartphone App for Treating Major Depression

While no direct source confirms an FDA‑cleared AI‑driven smartphone app for major depression, recent approvals and research show growing interest in AI‑based digital mental health tools.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

We examined the supplied FDA documents, news articles, clinical trial listings, and evidence reviews to assess the claim of an FDA‑approved AI‑driven smartphone app for major depression. The sources include FDA guidance on generative‑AI devices, announcements of cleared digital therapeutics, a trial record for AI‑guided CBT, and a 2026 review of mental health apps with clinical evidence.

The material does not contain a direct statement that the FDA has authorized an AI‑based passive‑sensing smartphone app for depression. Instead, it shows related developments: the FDA’s focus on generative‑AI mental health devices, the clearance of prescription digital therapeutics for depression, approval of a CBT‑based digital therapeutic for anxiety/depression, and an ongoing AI‑guided CBT study.

Assignment angle: This marks the first regulatory clearance of an AI‑based passive‑sensing therapeutic that uses real‑time phone data to deliver personalized CBT interventions without clinician involvement.

FDA’s Focus on Generative‑AI Digital Mental Health Devices

Source 1 indicates that the FDA’s Digital Health Advisory Committee (DHAC) is focusing on novel generative‑AI digital mental health medical devices. This signals regulatory attention to AI‑powered interventions in the mental health space.

Source 11 adds that mental health digital therapeutics have experienced particular growth, with FDA‑regulated software options now available for conditions such as depression, anxiety, PTSD, and substance use disorders.

Closing read

Conclusion

Factually, the supplied sources show that the FDA has cleared a prescription digital therapeutic for major depressive disorder (Rejoyn), approved a CBT‑based digital therapeutic for anxiety and depression (MindBalance), and is focusing its Digital Health Advisory Committee on generative‑AI mental health devices. An AI‑guided CBT program for mild depression and anxiety is under clinical investigation (NCT07576686).

The sources do not provide any evidence that the FDA has authorized an AI‑driven smartphone app that passively senses phone data to deliver personalized CBT for major depression without clinician involvement. Therefore, the headline claim remains unverified by the current material.

Looking forward, the regulatory emphasis on generative‑AI mental health tools, the established pathway for prescription DTx, and the accumulating data from AI‑CBT trials could support future submissions that combine passive sensing, AI algorithms, and CBT principles. Any such claim would require new FDA documentation not present in the supplied sources.
