How Law Firms Re-Run Cases Under Article 6a

The practical steps are emerging from best practices. Law firms should integrate bias audits into their case-management workflow, ideally before initial settlement offers are made. If a re-run is required, firms should recalculate offers using the corrected algorithm and then review the new distribution for disparate impact. The court-approved audit tools are designed to make this transparent—they can generate reports that compare settlement values by demographic group. Firms should also consider whether the original algorithm's vendor is liable, as the Workday ruling suggests. In practice, re-running a case may involve notifying affected claimants of the corrected offer, which can be sensitive but is increasingly expected by courts.

  • Re-run process: document audit findings, correct algorithm parameters, recompute offers for affected claimants.
  • Oversee the audit and re-run under legal counsel to preserve attorney-client privilege, per California ruling.
  • Review re-run results for disparate impact using court-approved audit tools.
  • Consider vendor liability for the original biased algorithm, as Workday precedent suggests.
  • Notify affected claimants of corrected offers to maintain compliance and trust.

Conclusion

The legal landscape is shifting: courts are allowing AI discrimination claims to proceed, and regulators are demanding bias audits. The Mobley v. Workday ruling is a concrete precedent that puts algorithm deployers on notice, and the EU AI Act Article 6a is a binding obligation for high-risk systems. For law firms, the message is clear: settlement algorithms are no longer black boxes. Court-approved audit tools can expose hidden demographic bias, and when they do, firms must re-run cases. This is not hypothetical—it is a foreseeable consequence of existing legal reasoning and regulatory pressure. However, the specific application to settlement algorithms remains an emerging area. No court has yet ordered a re-run under Article 6a, and the evidence base is thinner than in hiring. The safest course is proactive: integrate audits now, document everything, and treat bias correction as a legal duty. Firms that ignore this risk becoming the next defendant, while those that lead can turn compliance into a competitive advantage.
