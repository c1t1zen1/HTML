C2PA as the Baseline: How AI Video Watermarking Works

C2PA (Coalition for Content Provenance and Authenticity) provides a standardized method for embedding and verifying content credentials across images, video, and audio. According to source 1, C2PA cryptographically signs capture devices, records every edit, embeds invisible AI watermarks, and gives platforms and journalists tools to restore trust. Source 10 explains that OpenAI used C2PA metadata to tag Sora videos, and that C2PA metadata is central to AI video detection in 2026. Source 9 adds that tech companies rely on C2PA, cryptographic signing, and invisible watermarks like Google's SynthID, but also acknowledges real-world limitations of invisible AI watermarks.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence shows that C2PA watermarking is a central, standardized mechanism for AI video provenance, but it faces a growing adversarial wave of removal tools and techniques. Legal frameworks like California SB 942 and the EU AI Act are pushing for mandatory compliance, yet the same tools that strip C2PA metadata undermine those legal requirements. Sources conflict on the robustness of invisible watermarks: some suggest they are effective, while others detail how they are defeated. What is clear is that the battle is not purely technical—it is a legal and policy fight over what constitutes acceptable provenance and who can strip it. Projection: as enforcement deadlines arrive in 2026, we can expect more sophisticated removal tools and more aggressive platform countermeasures, but the fundamental tension between watermarking and adversarial removal will persist.
