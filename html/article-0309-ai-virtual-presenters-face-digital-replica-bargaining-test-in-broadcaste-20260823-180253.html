Deployment turns a presenter into a continuing rights relationship

The commercial proposition is no longer limited to generating a generic synthetic host. AI Studios describes custom avatars that reproduce a real presenter's appearance, voice, and gestures, and says MBN, CCTV, and Beijing TV have deployed custom AI anchors. If accurate, those deployments create an ongoing relationship between broadcaster, performer, and replica rather than a one-time production transaction.

That relationship makes the contract operational. A broadcaster needs to know whether the replica may deliver only approved scripts, whether new material requires additional approval, and what happens when the performer withdraws or suspends consent. The official SAG-AFTRA timeline confirms that recent bargaining is addressing those control questions, although the supplied evidence does not identify the terms of any named broadcaster's agreement.

Consent is moving from permission to continuing control

The strongest evidence supports consent as a continuing contractual issue, not merely an initial release. SAG-AFTRA says its Interactive Media Agreement includes consent and disclosure requirements and allows performers to suspend consent for generating new material during a strike. A legal summary likewise describes vocal and visual digital replicas as extensions of the performer's performance that are tightly regulated, with consent and bargaining generally required for other identified digital-replica uses.

A separate commentary source says identifiable-person replicas require consent regardless of union status under right-of-publicity regimes and newer AI-specific extensions. Another secondary summary reports that a foreign-language dubbing carve-out remains in a 2026 contract. Because the supplied material does not include the underlying agreements or statutory text, the breadth of those protections and exceptions should be verified before being treated as universal rules.

Closing read

Conclusion

The evidence supports a clear direction: virtual presenters modeled on identifiable people are making consent, disclosure, payment, and likeness-control terms practical deployment requirements. The official SAG-AFTRA material confirms recent bargaining activity on those subjects, while legal and industry summaries point to payment standards and bargaining requirements for different forms of digital replica.

What happens next remains partly a projection. Broadcaster expansion may increase pressure to define approval rights, reuse compensation, suspension mechanisms, and exceptions such as foreign-language dubbing.
