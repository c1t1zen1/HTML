California Enacts First State Law Mandating Deepfake Watermarking in Political Ads

California's new watermark rule for AI political ads sets a national precedent as courts weigh First Amendment limits.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

California enacted the first state law requiring visible and machine-readable watermarks on AI-generated political advertisements, effective July 2026, to combat election disinformation. This development reflects a growing state-level response to synthetic media threats, with at least 19 states having some form of deepfake regulation targeting political ads, though definitions and enforcement vary widely.

Assignment angle: California has become the first U.S. state to require visible and machine-readable watermarks on all AI-generated political advertisements, effective July 2026, to combat election disinformation.

FIELD NOTE

Watermark Requirements Under SB 942

The California AI Transparency Act (SB 942) mandates that developers of large generative AI systems implement both visible and hidden watermarks on AI-generated images, video, and audio content. The law requires companies building AI tools to add a hidden marker to their outputs and offer users a visible label. This dual-layer approach aims to help users identify synthetic media while enabling technical detection. The law took effect in 2026 and applies specifically to content created by covered AI systems.

  • SB 942 requires both visible labels and hidden markers on AI-generated content
  • The law applies to developers of large generative AI systems
  • Watermarks must be applied to images, video, and audio outputs
  • Companies must provide tools for users to access visible disclosures

Legal Challenges and First Amendment Tensions

California’s deepfake political ad regulations face ongoing First Amendment challenges, with courts having struck down similar laws in Hawaii and California for lacking exemptions for satire. The Attorney General is currently appealing to reinstate a deepfake ban in political ads, arguing the laws are necessary to defend democracy. These legal battles highlight the tension between regulating harmful synthetic media and protecting free speech, particularly for parody and political commentary.

Conclusion

California’s SB 942 represents a novel regulatory approach by mandating technical watermarks on AI-generated political ads, a measure designed to improve transparency and traceability of synthetic media. The law took effect in 2026 and requires both visible labels and hidden markers from AI developers, though its application to political ads is currently under legal scrutiny due to First Amendment concerns. The state’s effort highlights the difficulty of regulating emerging media threats without infringing on protected speech, particularly in political contexts.
