AI Crypto Bots Turn Profitable—Yet All 13 Lose to Bitcoin in Live Benchmark

TradeRank’s August live snapshot shows 10 of 13 identical-rule LLM traders in profit, but none outperforming Bitcoin, against an eight-season record with just 39% profitable model-seasons.

Editor’s brief

Why this matters now

The supplied TradeRank evidence shows a split result rather than a simple win for AI trading bots. Ten of 13 models were profitable in the live snapshot, yet zero surpassed Bitcoin, whose benchmark return was reported at +10.08% in the index-race summary. In other words, “lose to Bitcoin” means underperforming the benchmark, not necessarily posting a negative return.

The analysis combines TradeRank’s live leaderboard and daily-log snippets with its longer-run summary and settled Season 6 report. The material is snapshot-based and does not provide complete per-model returns, trading costs, risk measures, or the full rulebook. One supplied daily-log snippet also reports Bitcoin at both +10.08% and +7.83% under different measurement descriptions; the evidence does not reconcile those figures, so the comparison is stated as TradeRank reports it.

Assignment angle: TradeRank’s live Season 8 snapshot is distinct because it compares 13 identical-rule LLM traders against Bitcoin while its broader eight-season record shows only 39% of model-seasons finished profitable.

FIELD NOTE

Profitability did not equal benchmark victory

TradeRank’s August live competition placed 13 AI models under identical trading rules. The daily-log snapshot said none of the 13 models were beating Bitcoin, while 10 of 13 were in profit. That establishes the central distinction: positive absolute returns can coexist with relative underperformance.

The comparison depends on baselines. TradeRank’s leaderboard snippet says model returns began at the season open on August 15, 2026, while Bitcoin used an August 16 baseline and the S&P 500 an August 17 baseline. The supplied evidence therefore supports the direction of the result, but readers should not treat every displayed percentage as if it shared one starting date.

  • 10 of 13 models were reported profitable.
  • 0 of 13 models were reported ahead of Bitcoin in the daily index race.
  • “Losing to Bitcoin” does not mean all 13 models lost money.

FIELD NOTE

The live result is a relative-performance test

Bitcoin is the meaningful hurdle in this snapshot because it represents a passive alternative to the model portfolios. A model can finish above zero and still fail the test if Bitcoin gained more over the applicable comparison period.

The same source material reports that 12 of 13 models were beating the S&P 500 in the August 20 index race, while none beat Bitcoin. That contrast suggests the market regime and benchmark selection materially affect the apparent success of an AI strategy. It does not establish that the models have a durable edge over either asset.

  • Absolute profit and benchmark outperformance measure different outcomes.
  • The S&P 500 comparison was more favorable to the models than the Bitcoin comparison.
  • The supplied percentages use benchmark-specific baseline dates.

FIELD NOTE

Eight seasons temper the headline

TradeRank’s broader summary says 49 AI models traded across eight seasons beginning in January 2026, producing 2,724 trades and $900,000 of simulated capital, with 39% of model-seasons profitable. That record is materially less conclusive than a headline focused on the ten currently profitable models.

The settled Season 6 report shows why. Kimi K2.7 Code won that season at +2.14% with the field’s best realized profit and loss of +$233 across 13 trades, but all 11 models finished between +2.14% and -4.08%, and only four finished positive. TradeRank itself characterizes that as one settled season rather than a permanent verdict.

  • The longer record reports 39% profitable model-seasons, not consistent profitability.
  • Season 6 had a narrow winning margin and only 4 of 11 positive models.
  • A single season winner is not evidence of a permanent best model.

FIELD NOTE

What the evidence does—and does not—prove

The current snapshot supports a cautious claim: some LLM traders were profitable under TradeRank’s live rules, but none had yet demonstrated superiority to Bitcoin in the cited daily comparison. Earlier TradeRank evidence also reported that three of 12 models were above Bitcoin in Season 7, but none could hold that lead, indicating that relative leadership may be unstable.

The result should not be generalized to every AI trading service. A separate TradingArena.ai leaderboard describes a different competition in which Llama 4 Maverick was leading. That report is not evidence that the model leads TradeRank, because the supplied material does not establish that the two platforms use identical participants, dates, rules, or baselines.

  • TradeRank’s evidence supports a live snapshot conclusion, not a universal ranking of AI trading systems.
  • Earlier season evidence suggests that beating Bitcoin may be difficult to sustain.
  • Results from another leaderboard should remain separate unless methodologies are shown to match.

Closing read

Conclusion

The strongest fact in the supplied evidence is the gap between absolute and relative performance: TradeRank reported 10 profitable models, but zero of 13 ahead of Bitcoin in the cited live snapshot. The broader record is more restrained, with only 39% of model-seasons finishing profitable across eight seasons. Season 6 further shows that even a winning model can lead by a small margin while most of the field remains unprofitable.

Forecast: if future daily cycles and settled seasons repeatedly show models beating Bitcoin after comparable baselines, the case for durable AI trading skill would strengthen. If profitable counts remain high while Bitcoin continues to outperform, the more defensible conclusion will be that the bots can produce gains in some conditions without delivering a reliable passive-market edge.
