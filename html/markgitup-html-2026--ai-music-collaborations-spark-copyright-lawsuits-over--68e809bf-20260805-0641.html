Key Insights & Concepts

Fair Use as the Decisive Factor

Courts are uniformly analyzing the four fair use factors — purpose, nature, amount, and market effect — with particular focus on whether AI training is transformative and whether it usurps the market for original works.

Industry-Specific Implications

Outcomes will reshape licensing norms: music cases may affect royalty structures; publishing rulings could redefine training data agreements; visual art decisions may impact style mimicry and derivative work claims.

Training Data vs. Output Infringement

Litigation splits between challenges to the training process (input infringement) and claims that AI outputs substantially resemble protected expression (output infringement), requiring distinct legal analyses.

Global Legal Divergence

While U.S. courts focus on fair use, EU jurisdictions are examining compliance with the AI Act and text-and-data mining exceptions, creating potential conflicts in cross-border AI deployment and liability.

Takeaways & Next Steps

Key Takeaways from 2026 Litigation Trends

The NYT v. OpenAI case is the most closely watched bellwether; a ruling against fair use could trigger widespread licensing requirements for LLMs trained on published text.

Music industry cases (Suno, Udio, Anthropic) are testing whether AI-generated melodies and harmonies infringe on compositional copyright, with fair use defenses hinging on transformative output.

Settlements are increasing as parties seek to avoid uncertain rulings, often involving licensing fees, revenue sharing, or data deletion agreements rather than clear legal precedents.

DMCA 1202 claims (removal of copyright management information) are gaining traction as a complementary legal theory when fair use defenses fail, particularly in image and code generation cases.

Recommended Next Steps for Stakeholders

  1. Monitor summary judgment motions in NYT v. OpenAI and Concord Music Group v. Anthropic for early fair use indicators.
  2. Review internal AI training data provenance and document licensing efforts to mitigate infringement risk.
  3. Engage legal counsel to assess exposure under both fair use and DMCA 1202 theories based on content type and model use case.
  4. Participate in industry-led licensing initiatives (e.g., music collective agreements, news publisher consortia) to shape equitable training data frameworks.
  5. Prepare for potential output filtering, attribution requirements, or model retraining if courts find infringement in training or generation phases.
