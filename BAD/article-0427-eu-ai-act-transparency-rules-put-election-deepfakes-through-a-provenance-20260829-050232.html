The Provenance-and-Disclosure Test

A substantiated assessment would need evidence showing whether synthetic election content carries reliable provenance information from creation through distribution. It would also need to establish whether that information survives editing, reposting, platform transfer, and other forms of redistribution.

The disclosure component requires separate verification: a rule may require notice without making origin traceable, while provenance may exist without being visible or understandable to voters. The supplied material does not establish whether either mechanism works, who operates it, or how failures are detected.

  • Traceability and disclosure are distinct questions and should not be treated as interchangeable.
  • Technical provenance evidence would need to be matched with evidence about public visibility and comprehension.
  • Operational claims require documented examples, compliance records, or enforcement evidence that are absent here.
