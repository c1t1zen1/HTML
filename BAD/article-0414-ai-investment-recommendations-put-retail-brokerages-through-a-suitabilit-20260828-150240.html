FIELD NOTE

Suitability Requires Product-Level Evidence

The assignment raises whether AI stock picks in consumer brokerage products should be assessed against suitability expectations. The supplied material does not define those expectations or show whether any product considers an investor's objectives, risk tolerance, financial circumstances, knowledge, or intended use.

A defensible analysis would compare the recommendation's stated scope with the information collected from the customer and the warnings supplied before reliance. It would also examine whether the product distinguishes general education from individualized guidance. No supplied source shows that such distinctions are made or omitted.

  • Identify whether recommendations are general, personalized, or presented in an ambiguous middle ground.
  • Compare the system's claimed purpose with the customer information used to generate a pick.
  • Check whether limitations and intended audience are visible before a user acts.
FIELD NOTE

Provenance and Disclosure Are Separate Tests

An AI recommendation can raise a provenance question even when its wording includes a disclaimer. Readers need to know, at minimum, whether the output is based on current or historical information, identifiable analysis, generated reasoning, or an undisclosed combination of inputs. The supplied evidence does not answer any of these questions.

Disclosure also concerns presentation: whether users can recognize machine-generated content, understand material limitations, distinguish analysis from prediction, and see when information was produced or updated. These are review criteria, not findings supported by the missing source record.

  • Trace each recommendation to its disclosed data inputs, analytical method, and update process.
  • Check whether AI involvement, uncertainty, conflicts, and limitations are prominent and comprehensible.
  • Separate a disclosure that exists from a disclosure that is sufficiently specific to inform user judgment.
FIELD NOTE

What Would Resolve the Test

The next evidence set should contain primary materials from the relevant brokerage products, including onboarding screens, recommendation examples, disclosures, methodology descriptions, terms of service, and records showing how recommendations are monitored or corrected. Independent analysis could then test whether the public description matches the user experience.

Until those materials are supplied, the strongest conclusion is narrow: the suitability-and-disclosure question is open. Any forecast about future product design, regulatory treatment, or investor benefit must remain explicitly labeled as a forecast rather than reported fact.

  • Obtain product screenshots or documentation showing the recommendation flow.
  • Collect the exact disclosure language attached to AI-generated stock picks.
  • Compare stated methodology with recommendation provenance and update timestamps.
  • Look for independent testing, complaints, corrections, or public responses.
