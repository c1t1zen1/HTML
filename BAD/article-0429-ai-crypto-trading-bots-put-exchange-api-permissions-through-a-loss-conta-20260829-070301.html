Controls That Require Testing

A properly sourced investigation should examine whether API credentials can be restricted to the minimum trading actions required by a bot. It should also verify whether account owners can block withdrawals independently of trading access and whether permissions can be changed without replacing the entire integration.

Emergency kill switches require separate scrutiny. Future sources should clarify whether a shutdown can be triggered quickly, who can trigger it, what orders or positions remain active, and whether the control works during exchange outages, API failures, or abnormal bot behavior. None of these points is established by the supplied material.

  • Verify permission granularity instead of assuming API access is all-or-nothing.
  • Verify withdrawal restrictions separately from trading permissions.
  • Test whether emergency shutdowns affect open orders and positions.
  • Check control behavior during degraded exchange or API conditions.
FIELD NOTE

What a Loss-Containment Test Would Show

A rigorous comparison would need primary documentation, incident reports, or other identifiable evidence showing the configured permissions before an event, the bot behavior during the event, the exchange response, and the resulting account impact. Without that chain, a control may exist on paper without demonstrated containment.

Evidence should also distinguish prevention from recovery. Blocking withdrawals may reduce one type of exposure, while canceling orders or disabling credentials may address another. The supplied material does not show whether any control has been deployed, triggered, or independently validated.

  • Separate documented capability from observed effectiveness.
  • Distinguish prevention, interruption, and recovery outcomes.
  • Require an auditable sequence from bot action to exchange response.
  • Treat all forward-looking claims as forecasts until sourced.
